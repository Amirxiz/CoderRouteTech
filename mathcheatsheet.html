1. Sigma Symbol ()

The Symbol () is a mathematical symbol used to represent summation, which is the process of adding a sequence of numbers together. Instead of writing every addition separately, Sigma notation provides a shorter and more organized way to represent the calculation.

EXAMPLE:

Upper bound: 3
5n
Lower bound: n = 1

5 * 1 = 5
5 * 2 = 10
5 * 3 = 15
5 + 10 + 15 = 30

AGAIN:

Upper bound: 5
2n²
Lower bound: n = 1

2(1²) = 2
2(2²) = 8
2(3²) = 18
2(4²) = 32
2(5²) = 50
2 + 8 + 18 + 32 + 50 = 110

The Sigma notation consists of three main components: the upper bound, which determines where the summation ends; the lower bound, which determines the starting value; and the expression to the right of the symbol, which determines what value is added at each step.

5. Integrals ()

The Integral Symbol () is a mathematical symbol used in calculus to represent integration. Integration is commonly used to calculate accumulated quantities, such as the area under a curve, and to find antiderivatives.

THE TWO MAIN TYPES:

• Definite Integral: Has upper and lower bounds and produces a numerical value when evaluated.
• Indefinite Integral: Represents a family of antiderivatives and includes a constant of integration, C.
